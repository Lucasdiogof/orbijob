# OrbiJob 5.0 — global search, relevance and catalogue quality

What 5.0 changes in how a profession is searched, how the app and the alerts stay identical, what was measured, and what is **not** solved.
Everything here is implemented and covered by automated tests; the one database change (migration 19) is applied to the OrbiJob project
(`rpmlfxwebnlxnwadyvle`). Progress log: [ORBIJOB_5_0_PROGRESS.md](ORBIJOB_5_0_PROGRESS.md). Master checklist of the whole product:
[ORBIJOB_MASTER_CHECKLIST.md](ORBIJOB_MASTER_CHECKLIST.md).

## 1. The problem 4.0 had

Measured on the real catalogue (public key, the same reads the app makes — `scripts/search-benchmark.mjs`, evidence in
`docs/evidence/search-benchmark-2026-10-13.txt`):

* A search was widened to the **other names of the profession as loose fragments anywhere in the title**. "engenheiro civil" became "any
  engineer" (849 results, none civil), "fisioterapia pélvica" became "any physiotherapist" (212 results, none pelvic), "desenvolvedor flutter"
  became "any developer" (140 results, none Flutter).
* Common trades had no other names at all (eletricista, soldador: 0 results although the catalogue has 11 electricians and 11 welders in Swedish,
  German and English).
* A word that is a profession in one language and something else in another ("chef" is a manager in Swedish) was treated as an equivalent.
* The alert matcher in SQL could not express "these two words must both be in the title", so alerts and the app could not agree.

## 2. What it does now

**Alternatives with required parts.** A search term that names a profession is widened to its other names. Each alternative is a *list of parts
that must all be in the title*; a single fragment is the one-part case. `civil engineer` stays `[civil, engineer]`, never `engineer`.
Implemented in `app/lib/features/search/domain/search_alternatives.dart`; both the catalogue query (`and(title.ilike.*a*,title.ilike.*b*)`
inside the `or`) and the saved-search `match.alts` use it.

**Two sources, both offline and deterministic**
* **ESCO** (European Skills, Competences, Qualifications and Occupations): 2,942 occupations downloaded on 2026-10-13, compiled to
  `occupation_labels.g.dart` — 2,853 occupations, 31,824 names in **pt, en, es, de, sv, nl, fr** (at most 3 per language and occupation, preferred
  first). When the typed term is a name of an occupation (also in the other grammatical gender: "enfermeira" / "enfermeiro"), the names that
  occupation has in the other languages become the alternatives. Rebuild: `node scripts/esco-fetch.mjs <dir>` (resumable, polite, 4 workers,
  backoff) then `node scripts/build-occupations.mjs <dir>/details.json`.
* **Hand-made groups** (`search_synonyms.dart`): stems (`fisioterap`, `pflege`, …) for professions ESCO splits into many specialisations or
  words people write in compounds. Words the person adds ("flutter", "civil") stay required; two matched groups ("fisioterapia" + "pélvica")
  are required together.

**What the table filters out** (false equivalences are worse than missing ones): alternative names that do not share the head word of the
preferred name (ESCO lists related jobs among alternatives); labels longer than 4 words or with punctuation; labels shared by more than 6
occupations; editorial markers between `« »`; a label that is the preferred name of exactly one occupation belongs to it alone ("electrician"
is the occupation Electrician, only a related name of "electrical mechanic"). Single words shorter than 6 letters never stand alone as another
profession's name (substring risk: "lasser", "cook" inside other words), and a small list of known false friends ("chef", "controller",
"revisor", …) never does either. The person's own typed word is always searched.

**A profession search is judged on the title.** A term that has alternatives matches the title or the city only; a description that merely
mentions the word is no longer a result. Any other term keeps the full-text match on title, company and description.

**Suggestions.** When nothing is found the empty state offers "Try instead": the same profession without the words that narrowed it
("fisioterapia pélvica" → "fisioterapia") or professions spelled almost like the term ("electricain" → "electrician"). They are only searches the
person may choose.

**Attribution.** ESCO is CC BY 4.0: the generated file's header, `docs/OCCUPATIONS.md` and the app's licence page carry it.

## 3. App ↔ alerts parity

`ops.alert_matches` (migration `20261026000000_search_alternatives.sql`, **applied**) accepts `match.alts` entries that are strings or arrays of
1–6 strings, and applies the title-only rule to searches with alternatives. `worker/test/fixtures/v3-specs.golden.json` is generated by the
Dart reference (`flutter test test/searches/search_spec_test.dart`, `UPDATE_GOLDEN=1`) and the SQL function must answer exactly the same for
every search × job pair (PGlite in `worker/test/v3-alerts.test.ts`, real PostgreSQL 16/17 in CI). The golden now includes qualified professions
(`engenheiro civil`, `desenvolvedor flutter`, `eletricista`, `fisioterapia pélvica`) against jobs such as *Bauingenieur*, *Civil Engineer*,
*Mechanical Engineer*, *Senior Flutter-Entwickler*, *Java Developer*, *Kfz-Elektriker*, *Electrical Engineer*, *Beckenbodenphysiotherapeut*.
After applying, the function was also run on the real catalogue: `engenheiro civil` → 0 (the app shows 0), `eletricista` → 11 (the app shows 11).
The recommender uses the same resolver for "same profession in another language".

## 5. Known limits

* A qualifier that needs translating is not translated: "truck driver" finds `truck` + `fahrer/motorista/conductor` titles, not "Lastbilschaufför".
  ESCO covers the qualified occupations it lists ("civil engineer" → "Bauingenieur"); anything else falls back to the typed words.
* Matching is substring-in-title, accent-sensitive, as in the SQL (`ilike`). A short stem can sit inside another word.
* ESCO names are not reviewed by native speakers; professions and regulated titles are **not** equated by law — a name match says nothing about
  registration, visas or recognition abroad.
* No fuzzy matching in the catalogue query itself; spelling help exists only in the empty-state suggestions.

## 6. Catalogue coverage and health (read-only diagnostic)

`npx supabase db query --linked -o csv -f scripts/supabase/diagnostics/coverage.sql`; the run of 2026-10-13 is kept in
`docs/evidence/coverage-2026-10-13.csv`.

* 6,694 open jobs, 6,694 visible (verified in the last 72 h): jobtech 2,364 (SE), arbeitnow 1,800, twjobs 1,090 (TW), jobicy 988, jobsconz 332
  (NZ), remoteok 102, remotive 18.
* By country: SE 2,354 · TW 1,090 · US 434 · NZ 335 · GB 83 · DE 79 · FR 65 · CH 36 · CA 31 · AU 30 … **BR 11, PT 11, MX 13, ES 9**:
  Latin America is thin because no connector with Latin-American inventory is published yet (a source problem, not a search one).
* 2,009 jobs state no single country (Arbeitnow lists cities only; 170 were resolved in 4.0 where the employer's location ends with a country).
* 609 descriptions under 200 characters, 265 jobs published over 60 days ago but still confirmed open, 4 fingerprints shared by two sources
  (the discovery refuses a second copy; two different jobs with one title and company stay apart).
* Failures in 7 days: Arbeitnow 2 × `catalogue_full` (before the ceiling went from 1,500 to 2,500), Jobicy 1 × `max_pages`; every other run `ok`.
* Database 118 MB of 500 MB.
